# Commission edits instead of harvesting templates

**Opening CapCut templates in CapCut and reading them with rea-capcut does not give you exact labels, and it does not mean "we can do anything" with the result.** Technically, rea-capcut's JianYing import is experimental and lossy. It drops transitions, effects, keyframes, curve speed and templates, which are the fields your label JSON needs ([CAPCUT_INTEROP.md](https://github.com/Xiangyu-CAS/rea-capcut/blob/main/docs/CAPCUT_INTEROP.md)). REA is a general reverse-engineering toolkit with no CapCut support ([REA README](https://github.com/morluto/rea)), and FilmCraft is an unrelated Premiere-style editor ([FilmCraft](https://github.com/storytold/filmcraft)). CapCut's own documentation says template drafts are locked unless the creator enabled paid unlock, and even then the creator's media stays protected ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/what-is-template-monetization)). Legally, rea-capcut is MIT-licensed code that licenses none of CapCut's effects, fonts, music or template content. Its README tells users to "use media you own or can redistribute" ([README](https://github.com/Xiangyu-CAS/rea-capcut)). The best-supported route to roughly 800 exact-label reels is to **author them yourselves in CapCut International on a pinned version, on footage and music you own**. You would parse the plaintext draft JSON with a mapper you write, and render the video with the same app. Programmatic draft generation fills any shortfall. The main open risks are three things nobody has tested yet. First, whether current CapCut drafts are still plain JSON. Second, whether a Materials-License carve-out or a written ByteDance permission is needed for commercial training. Third, whether CapCut is usable from India at all. This is research, not legal advice, and counsel should review before any scaled collection.

## The three tools you have do not form a pipeline

REA ("Reverse Engineer Anything") is an MCP server for analysing binaries, apps, websites and APKs. Its README never mentions CapCut, JianYing or video editors. Its only link to your problem is that rea-capcut's author used it to study JianYing ([REA README](https://github.com/morluto/rea)). It is a method for learning a draft schema, not a data tool. The notes could not verify its star count, so do not cite that number.

rea-capcut (ReaCut) is the only one of the three that touches drafts. It is an Electron editor at v0.7.1 preview, with a prebuilt DMG for Apple Silicon only. The README states that "Windows/Linux binaries are not provided, and existing Windows test failures remain" ([README](https://github.com/Xiangyu-CAS/rea-capcut)). Its interop document claims a decoder for JianYing 11.x drafts: plaintext "classic" JSON, plus AES-256-GCM payloads whose key and IV are derived from twelve shards inside the payload. That decoder was verified on two controlled projects of a single build, JianYing 11.0.12922. The same document lists keyframes, curve speed, complex effects, templates, transitions, cropping and reverse playback as **not mapped**, and says there is no write-back ([CAPCUT_INTEROP.md](https://github.com/Xiangyu-CAS/rea-capcut/blob/main/docs/CAPCUT_INTEROP.md)). Its export renders from ReaCut's own engine on the lossy import, so the video would not match what CapCut would render. One internal inconsistency remains unresolved. An earlier REA findings note says the research did not identify the encryption scheme ([REA_FINDINGS.md](https://github.com/Xiangyu-CAS/rea-capcut/blob/main/docs/research/REA_FINDINGS.md)), while the later interop doc describes a working decoder. I could not test the decoder against real drafts.

FilmCraft is an alpha Rust clean-room reimplementation of Premiere Pro. It reads FCP7 XML, FCPXML, OTIO, EDL and AAF, and never mentions CapCut or JianYing ([README](https://github.com/storytold/filmcraft)). OpenTimelineIO would be a poor label format anyway. It has no effect or text model, and capcut-cli's OTIO export drops text tracks by default ([capcut-cli CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/renezander030/capcut-cli/master/CHANGELOG.md)). The only useful artefact among the three is ReaCut's interop document, as a reference for draft structure. A custom JSON mapper from the draft schema is the right label target.

## Templates are the wrong door: mobile-first, locked, and label-free

The assumption behind the template route is that applying a trending template yields a normal draft with the full edit inside. CapCut's help pages do not support that. One help page says "the desktop version does not support accessing the CapCut template library," and that desktop has only a few official templates ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/use-and-export-templates-in-capcut)). A page dated 12 January 2026 describes a desktop Templates tab and warns that "the creator's original media … may be protected" ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/use-template)). The notes could not reconcile the two. Another page says some templates "won't open on the desktop version" and suggests exporting on a phone ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/open-a-template-in-capcut-pc)). Creators can enable paid unlock ("Edit More"), and "if the creator has not enabled monetization, users cannot edit the draft" ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/what-is-template-monetization)). One secondary source claims template-library downloads carry an encrypted `draft_info.json` ([capcut-editor skill](https://www.skills.sh/duckyman-ai/agent-skills/capcut-editor)). It names no version, so treat it as unconfirmed. Mobile drafts are not stored as an accessible `draft_content.json`, so a mobile template run yields an MP4 and no labels.

JianYing has the same structure. Old but consistent community reports describe template (模板) drafts as apply-only until paid unlock, with only editable (剪辑) drafts syncing to PC ([Toutiao](https://www.toutiao.com/zixun/7503702163357452322/)). Template discovery also has no official API. The public gallery exposes preview pages and usage counts, and third-party scrapers exist but are unverified ([hooked.so guide](https://www.hooked.so/capcut-templates), [Apify actor](https://apify.com/logiover/capcut-templates-downloader)). Scripting against it runs into the ToS clause discussed below. Together this means template use gives you looks without labels, or labels only for locked, partly encrypted content that the Materials License puts out of bounds. The academic precedent points the same way. AutoTransition built its set from public editing-software templates but released only rendered videos and per-transition category and timing, with no licence statement on the underlying templates ([arXiv 2207.13479](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.13479)). Even its authors did not redistribute the source drafts.

## Plaintext drafts are the real label source, with version drift to manage

A readable draft is itself the exact timeline. The capcut-cli draft-schema documents show that segments carry `target_timerange`, transitions sit on the outgoing segment's `extra_material_refs[2]`, text animations live in `material_animations`, effects and filters in `video_effects` and `effects`, and keyframes in `common_keyframes` and `keyframe_graph_list`. Speed is a material attached to each segment ([schema overview](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/capcut-cli-david@1.14.1/docs/draft-schema/00-overview.md), [version differences](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/capcut-cli-david@2.5.0/docs/draft-schema/05-version-differences.md)). Cuts, transitions, text animation, effects, speed, camera moves (keyframes plus clip transform), layout (canvas and transform) and colour (filters and adjustments) all have a source field. The notes did not read the per-material field docs, so the exact JSON keys are unconfirmed. No existing tool converts a draft to your label schema, so that mapper is custom glue. It also needs per-version tables mapping resource IDs to human names, because drafts carry IDs and CapCut International and JianYing use different libraries. The notes did not establish whether CapCut or pyCapCut can dump a complete parsed timeline.

**The version story decides feasibility.** JianYing 6.0 and later encrypt the draft, and CapCut International is reported plain JSON. The maintainers of capcut-cli chose "detect, do not decrypt" ([jianying-encryption.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/renezander030/capcut-cli/master/docs/jianying-encryption.md)). Fixtures cover CapCut 8.x (mac 8.2.0, Windows 8.5.0 and 8.6.0) ([COMPATIBILITY.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/capcut-cli-david@2.5.0/COMPATIBILITY.md)). The file that holds the timeline moves between versions. CapCut 9.2.8-beta4 on macOS reads `Timelines/<id>/draft_info.json` and treats the root files as legacy mirrors, and CapCut 10.x is unconfirmed with no fixture ([version-support.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/renezander030/capcut-cli/master/docs/version-support.md)). pyJianYingDraft's own README now says new-version drafts are "often not directly readable plain JSON" ([GuanYixuan/pyJianYingDraft](https://github.com/GuanYixuan/pyJianYingDraft)). The newest source I read on CapCut International dates from May 2026, so the current state is untested. Pin a version, disable auto-update, and verify per install.

**Rendering is the second constraint.** pyJianYingDraft's batch export is Windows-only, works up to JianYing 6.8, and needs an export-permitted account. pyCapCut says "Automated export is currently unsupported" ([PyPI pycapcut](https://pypi.org/project/pycapcut/)). Generators (pyJianYingDraft, pyCapCut, VectCutAPI and CapCut Mate) write drafts with real effect, transition and animation enums, so labels are exact by construction. The notes found no measured throughput for any of them. Public datasets supplement single tracks only. Nothing public provides full reel labels. AutoTransition (34,998 videos, about 138.8k transitions, tagged cc-by-4.0) covers transitions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.13479), [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/yaojie-shen/AutoTransition)). Edit3K (618,800 single-component renders, a gated HF dataset with no licence, MIT code) covers effect, animation, transition, filter, sticker and text appearance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16048v2), [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Gstar666/Edit3K)). SHOT/AutoShot (853 short videos) and ClipShots (4,039 videos) cover cuts ([AutoShot](https://github.com/wentaozhu/AutoShot), [ClipShots](https://github.com/Tangshitao/ClipShots)). The cc-by-4.0 tag on AutoTransition does not clear the underlying third-party template renders. Edit3K was rendered in CapCut over ImageNet footage, so commercial use is unresolved and a written query to the authors is the practical fix.

## "We can do anything" fails on three separate layers

Treat tool legality, app terms and training-law exposure as three distinct questions. The first is who made the tool. rea-capcut's authors say they did "independently implemented" research limited to "authorized scope" ([README](https://github.com/Xiangyu-CAS/rea-capcut)). Whether that is lawful interoperability work is their risk. The 17 USC 1201(f) interoperability exception covers analysis "for the sole purpose" of making an independent program interoperate, not extracting content for a dataset ([17 USC ch.12](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap12.html)). Reading a plain JSON draft is not circumvention, but decrypting a protected one would implicate 1201(a). That is a reason to stay out of the JianYing decryption path.

The second layer is the terms of the app you use. CapCut's ToS (last updated 15 April 2026, Singapore law) bars reverse engineering and any use of "automated scripts or other technologies to collect information from or otherwise interact with the Services" ([ToS](https://www.capcut.com/clause/terms-of-service)). The Materials License (22 January 2026) is the most dangerous document. It bars editing Platform Materials "outside the Platform" and reverse engineering them, and licenses them per exported content, often as non-commercial only. Mixing in non-commercial materials limits the whole export to non-commercial use ([Materials License](https://www.capcut.com/clause/materials-license-agreement)). Neither document mentions AI training, but silence is not permission, because the licences are "limited" and "revocable". The notes read only snippets of both documents, and the Chinese JianYing agreement was not retrieved. In the US, a contractual reverse-engineering ban can bind even where copyright law would not (Bowers v. Baystate, as reported by [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/publications/shrink-wrap-licenses-and-reverse-engineering-april-16-2003)). Whether a Singapore-law online ToS would be enforced against an Indian company is uncertain.

The third layer is what training does under copyright law. India has an interim Delhi High Court ruling (24 July 2026) that prima facie treats storing works to train a model as fair dealing, which does not bind the merits ([SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2026/07/training-chatgpt-on-copyrighted-works-prima-facie-protected-under-indian-copyright-act-delhi-high-court-refuses-interim-injunction-against-openai.html)). A DPIIT working paper proposes mandatory licensing with royalties on commercialisation, limited to lawfully accessed works ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1718746/exploring-the-dpiits-working-paper-on-generative-ai-and-copyright)). In the US, Thomson Reuters v. Ross rejected fair use for a non-generative competing tool ([OSU Libraries](https://library.osu.edu/site/copyright/2026/03/20/fair-use-and-artificial-intelligence-2026-update/)). A model that generates edit templates arguably competes with CapCut templates, which makes it look more like Ross than like a general LLM. That is the notes' assessment, not a holding. No case law was found on copyright in editing templates or editing style. Cut timing is probably an unprotectable idea, while a specific template's footage, music and distinctive assets are the protectable parts. Data acquired by automation, cache extraction or decryption gives a weak "lawful access" story under all three regimes.

## Ranked data plan: own the edit, then own the render

The ranking below is mine, built from the notes' risk ordering. Effort figures are my estimates, since the notes found no measured throughput, and the pilot below should replace them. "Real" here means real app output with human editing decisions, not reels found in the wild. No route found yields 800 wild reels with exact labels and clean rights.

| Rank | Route | Effort | Label quality | Legal risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Commission authored reels: contractors or employees with signed IP assignment edit your own or licensed footage in pinned CapCut International; you parse the plaintext draft and use the app's export | High human time. About 0.5 to 1.5 person-hours per reel (guess), so roughly 400 to 1,200 hours for 800 | Exact for everything the draft encodes; needs a resource-ID name table | Low to medium. Use no music and, ideally, only Dual Use materials; get ByteDance written confirmation for commercial training use |
| 2 | Generated drafts rendered in the app: pyCapCut or pyJianYingDraft with a parameter distribution tuned to reel grammar; render with CapCut (manual or custom UI automation) or JianYing 6.8 or earlier (scripted) | Medium engineering; export is the bottleneck | Exact by construction; may look synthetic | Low to medium. UI automation sits near ToS s.6 item 9; Materials License applies per export |
| 3 | Creator-licensed reels: paid, consenting creators supply export plus plaintext draft | Medium; recruitment and contracts | Exact if the draft is readable | Low if contracts cover training |
| 4 | PVE's own renderer (your 200) | Already planned | Exact | Lowest |
| 5 | Public datasets as sub-track supplements (AutoTransition, Edit3K, SHOT, ClipShots) | Low | Single-track, not reel-like | Medium to high; licences unstated or unclear, ask authors first |
| 6 | Template route (open template in the app, read with rea-capcut) | High, unreliable | Poor: locked drafts, lossy import | High (Materials License, automation) |
| 7 | Downloading creators' reels, VLM or human labelling | Medium | Approximate | Highest: third-party footage and music, host-platform ToS |

A sensible mix for the first run is 300 to 500 reels from rank 1 plus rank 3, 300 to 500 from rank 2 to reach 800 app-rendered reels, and 200 from rank 4. Do not mix route 7 into training. If you want a wild-reel eval set, use only creator-consented reels or have counsel clear it first. Keep a dated provenance log per sample covering source, editor, version, assets used and licence.

## First steps to verify, in order

1. **Plain JSON test (half a day).** Install CapCut International desktop 8.x, disable auto-update, and build one reel with a transition, a text in/out animation, an effect, a filter, a speed curve and keyframes. Run `json.loads` on `draft_content.json`, `draft_info.json`, `template-2.tmp` and `Timelines/*/draft_info.json`. Repeat on the latest 9.x or 10.x build. Record which parses, and which file is the source of truth.
2. **Template lock test.** In your own account, manually open three to five templates (desktop Templates tab if it exists, otherwise via phone cloud sync). Check whether a draft folder appears, whether it parses, whether "Edit More" unlock changes that, and whether the creator media is blank. This is manual only, with no scraping and no decryption. The expected result from the notes is locked or unparseable.
3. **Schema mapping spike.** Read the draft with capcut-cli (`info`, `segments`, `texts`) and read the schema files the notes did not (`03-keyframes-and-animations.md`, `04-effects-filters-stickers.md`). Write a mapper for each label field on the step 1 draft, and check whether the draft carries human-readable names or only IDs.
4. **Render fidelity check.** Export from the app, extract frames, and compare detected cut times against labels. Also check whether effects that need cloud downloads render correctly in batch.
5. **Throughput pilot.** Have two editors produce 20 reels and time them. This replaces the effort guesses above. Time a generated-draft batch the same way.
6. **Legal pack.** Archive dated snapshots of the CapCut ToS and Materials License. Get the JianYing agreement read in Chinese. Confirm that CapCut can be lawfully installed and used from India. My recollection, not covered in the notes, is that it has been blocked there for years. Engage counsel (India, plus Singapore for the ToS) and ask ByteDance for a written data-use permission. Email the Edit3K and AutoTransition authors about dataset licensing.
7. **Settle on the contractor and IP paperwork** before step 5, so pilot reels can enter the corpus.

## Conclusion

The notes change the question from "which tool extracts labels" to "who owns the edit". Labels are free only when a draft is readable and the edit is yours, and that condition rules out locked templates and wild reels. The tools matter less than a pinned CapCut version, a mapper you write, and rights you hold.

The open-source gaps are narrower than they look. The schema documents and pyCapCut or pyJianYingDraft generators exist, but export automation for CapCut International and draft readability on current builds are untested, and those two tests (steps 1 and 4) decide whether 800 reels is a months-long effort or a quarter-long one. The legal gap is wider: the Materials License's per-export, mostly non-commercial licensing is the clause that most threatens a commercial training set, even on your own footage. Settle it in writing with ByteDance before you scale, not after.
