# Free tiers cover most Reel editor development

The highest-quality Reel editor stack can be developed end to end at roughly zero cost, but only by splitting every layer into a free development key and a paid production key behind the same SDK, and by never letting real user footage touch a free key. Three things are not free: the Claude Sonnet 5.5 / Haiku 5.5 planner (no free variant exists, so budget a one-time **$10 OpenRouter top-up**), generative B-roll (no verified free credits on fal.ai, Replicate, Runway, Luma or Kling), and any production-grade GPU or FFmpeg render host. TwelveLabs, the service you named, has a Free plan of **3 GB cumulative storage plus an unpublished "Basic" Workload, with a hard stop and no overage**; existing free accounts keep the legacy **600-minute** cap until they migrate on **2027-01-04**, so TwelveLabs is a workable A/B arm for prototyping but its workload size cannot yet be turned into a number of test reels ([TwelveLabs pricing](https://www.twelvelabs.io/pricing), [migration doc](https://docs.twelvelabs.io/v1.3/docs/get-started/plans-and-billing/migrate-to-new-billing.md)). Gemini's free tier is the only free route with native video input, but on it Google may train on and human-review your content, and the terms carve out only the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, not India ([Gemini terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). Startup credits reachable without investors are few: Cloudflare Tier 3 ($10k), AWS Activate Founders ($1,000 to $5,000) and Microsoft's $1,000 to $5,000 offer, with Anthropic's program closed. **No note confirmed India availability, Indian-card payment or INR/GST handling for any service**, so every row below carries that open question.

Labelling: "Sourced" means a research note cites a URL for it; "Inference" is synthesis or arithmetic from the notes, not a vendor statement. Free tiers change monthly and all figures are as read on 2026-11-04.

## The quality stack and which parts cost money

**Sourced (earlier report).** The recommended stack is layered: TransNetV2/AutoShot (or PySceneDetect) for cut boundaries, PP-OCRv5 plus tracking for on-screen text, OpenCV for camera motion, Beat This!, WhisperX and python-audio-separator for audio, Gemini 3.8 Flash direct to Google for per-shot semantics, Marengo 3.5 or Gemini Embedding 2 for clip embeddings, Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Haiku 5.5 first pass) via OpenRouter for planning and chat revision, a Gemini Flash pairwise judge for verification, and FFmpeg plus libass for rendering through Kinocut (the renamed `mcp-video`, Apache-2.0), with HyperFrames (Apache-2.0) as an optional overlay layer. MCP stays off the runtime path: Kinocut is called through its Python `Client`, and community MCPs (Context7, Playwright, Chrome DevTools, Replicate, Hugging Face) are Claude Code development tools only ([earlier report](C:/Users/kiran/Desktop/PVE/reports/Reel%20editor%20model%20and%20tool%20stack.md)). Listed prices there: Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10 per million tokens, Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50, Gemini 3.8 Flash $0.75/$3.75 until 2026-12-31 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)).

**Inference.** Almost everything in the quality stack is open source and already free to run: TransNetV2, PySceneDetect, PP-OCRv5, Beat This!, WhisperX, audio-separator, FFmpeg, libass, Kinocut and HyperFrames cost only compute, and the MCP servers are free software whose hosted variants (TwelveLabs, Replicate, Hugging Face, ElevenLabs) simply draw on the underlying service's plan. The money sits in four hosted calls: the Gemini VLM and embeddings, the Claude planner, TwelveLabs, and optional generation. That is why the free-tier question reduces to those four plus compute and hosting. Claude Code's own free options were not researched.

## Compute, hosting and storage: only Modal is a free callable GPU

**Sourced, GPU.** Modal Starter gives **$30/month of free credits** with T4 at $0.000164/s, L4 $0.000222/s and A100-40 $0.000583/s, limited to 10 GPU concurrency and 100 containers; the pricing page did not say whether a card is required, and AWS/GCP/Azure credits cannot be applied to it ([Modal](https://modal.com/pricing)). Hugging Face ZeroGPU gives a free account **5 GPU-minutes/day** (2 unauthenticated) on Gradio only, up to two ZeroGPU Spaces ([HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu)). Colab free is variable and unpublished, Kaggle is commonly reported at about 30 GPU-hours/week but the quota is not in Kaggle's docs, SageMaker Studio Lab is closed to new customers, Lightning's terms conflict (headline "up to 30 starting credits", FAQ 5 on registration and 25 after adding a card), and Saturn Cloud and Paperspace are unconfirmed ([Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html), [gpuperhour](https://gpuperhour.com/blog/free-cloud-gpus-and-credits), [Lightning](https://lightning.ai/pricing)). Locally, WhisperX needs under 8 GB for large-v2, Beat This! checkpoints are about 78 MB, audio-separator supports CPU, and Qwen3-VL-8B at Q4_K_M is about 6 GB, though sources range from 4.8 to 12 GB; SAM 2 and FlashVSR are Linux/WSL and A100-class oriented ([WhisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX), [beat_this](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this), [Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/qwen3-vl-4b-vs-qwen3-vl-8b-benchmarks-vram-guide/), [SAM 2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2), [FlashVSR](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR)).

**Inference.** Modal's $30 buys about 37 L4-hours (30 / 0.000222 / 3600) or roughly 15 A100-40-hours a month, enough to exercise each GPU model one at a time with scale-to-zero functions, not to serve continuously. Kaggle's reported 130 hours/month is the biggest free GPU pool but is notebook-only, so use it for offline batches such as SigLIP 2 embeddings or Qwen3-VL captioning of a test library. Keep SAM 2 large, FlashVSR and Qwen3-VL-8B off the laptop critical path until measured on your actual card, which the notes never identified.

**Sourced, hosting.** There is no good always-on free FastAPI plus worker host. Oracle Always Free was cut effective 2026-06-15 to **2 OCPU / 12 GB Arm** (from 4/24), reclaims instances idle under 20% for seven days, and "out of host capacity" is common; Oracle signup needs a card ([Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm), [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/07/oracle-cloud-free-tier-limits/)). Cloud Run free is 2M requests, 180,000 vCPU-s and 360,000 GiB-s per month, no GPUs, card needed for billing ([GCP free](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)). Fly.io has no free tier; Render free web services sleep after 15 minutes, have no workers, and free Postgres expires after 30 days ([Fly](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing), [Render](https://render.com/docs/free)); Vercel Hobby is restricted to non-commercial personal use ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)); Hugging Face Docker and Gradio Spaces now need PRO, apart from the two ZeroGPU Spaces ([HF Spaces](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview)). Frontend and storage are better: Cloudflare Pages is free (500 builds/month) ([Pages](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)), **Cloudflare R2 gives 10 GB, 1M Class A and 10M Class B operations per month with zero egress** ([R2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)), Backblaze B2 10 GB ([B2](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing)), Neon 1 GB Postgres per project with no card ([Neon](https://neon.com/pricing)), and Turso 5 GB with 100 databases ([Turso](https://turso.tech/pricing)). Supabase's 50 MB upload cap rules it out for video ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)).

**Inference.** Two cheap Arm cores will render 1080p H.264 at roughly real time or slower (unbenchmarked), which is acceptable for a demo and not for concurrent users, and Cloud Run's 50 vCPU-hours would cover only a modest number of short renders. A reel editor can pass 10 GB of free R2 quickly, so delete renders after 24 to 72 hours. Develop on Windows locally and treat any free host as a demo.

## Verdict: what is enough and what blocks

**Inference from the sourced rows.** Free is enough to build and test the following end to end: transcription (Deepgram's $200 at $0.0043/min is about 46,500 minutes, and WhisperX runs locally for nothing), OCR (local PP-OCRv5, with Google, Azure or OCR.space as backups), shot detection and beats (open source, local), music ID (AudD's 300 requests is 300 reference reels at one request each), stock B-roll (Pexels and Pixabay), rendering (local FFmpeg and Kinocut), and video description plus embeddings (Gemini free key on non-sensitive clips). TwelveLabs Free can run an A/B arm on a handful of short reels; the notes' estimate is 10 to 60 reels per 3 GB depending on bitrate, which is an estimate, not a vendor number, and the Basic Workload size is unpublished. Memories.ai's $100 buys about 830 minutes of embedding at $0.12/min.

The blockers are specific. The planner has no free Sonnet or Haiku, but a $10 OpenRouter top-up also unlocks 1,000 free-model requests/day, and at the notes' ~10K-in/3K-out call, Haiku 5.5 costs about $0.0025 a call (roughly 4,000 calls per $10) while Sonnet 5.5 costs about $0.05 (roughly 200 calls). Generative fills have no free API route, so budget $10 to $20 or use self-hosted open models (a design suggestion, not sourced). Production use is blocked on Gemini free (training), ElevenLabs Free (non-commercial), Vercel Hobby (non-commercial), Fly.io (no free tier), HF Docker Spaces (PRO), and Render free (no workers, sleeping). Qwen-VL via Alibaba's free quota is closed to new accounts, so open-weight VLM tests run locally or on Modal. OpenRouter free tool-calling models cannot validate Sonnet's JSON-schema behaviour.

## A zero-cost development setup that mirrors production

**Inference (constructed from the findings above, not separately sourced).** Make every hosted dependency a config switch so production changes keys and endpoints, never code. The design rule is one adapter per layer with the same SDK, schema and model ID in both environments.

| Layer | Dev (free) | Production (paid) | Code change |
|---|---|---|---|
| VLM and judge | Gemini key from a separate no-billing project, `gemini-3.8-flash` or `3.5-flash-lite`, your own or licensed clips only | Billed project, same SDK and IDs, no training | Key only |
| Embeddings | Gemini Embedding 2 free key; SigLIP 2 locally | Billed project | Key only |
| Planner and chat | OpenRouter with `LLM_BASE_URL`, `LLM_MODEL`; `openrouter/free` for plumbing, pennies of Haiku 5.5 for real schema behaviour | Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 IDs | Model ID only |
| TwelveLabs arm | Free plan, same SDK | Plus or Pro | Plan only |
| ASR | Local WhisperX, Deepgram $200 as hosted check | Deepgram or Scribe | Provider flag |
| OCR | Local PP-OCRv5 | Same | None |
| Render | Local FFmpeg, libass, Kinocut `Client` | Same binary on a worker | None |
| GPU jobs | Modal functions ($30/month), Kaggle batches, local Ollama or LM Studio serving Qwen3-VL-8B Q4 on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint | Paid Modal or other | Env only |
| Storage and DB | R2 (S3 API) with 24-72 h TTL, SQLite or free Neon Postgres | Paid R2, Postgres | Connection string |
| Frontend and API | Local React and FastAPI; Cloudflare Pages; Oracle A1 for a demo | Commercial host | Deploy target |

Three disciplines make the swap safe: cache every model response by content hash so free RPD caps are not hit while iterating, record fixtures for unit tests, and build schema-validate-and-retry plus 429 backoff from day one because free tiers will trigger them. Run the same evaluation set on free and paid models before trusting dev results, since no source shows that free-tier models match production on JSON strictness. Keep Gemini's free project strictly unbilled, because linking billing converts the whole project to paid tier. Never send third-party reference reels or user footage through a free key, since the Gemini terms allow human review and training.

## Conclusion

The practical insight is that the cost problem is smaller than it looks, because the free tiers cover the deterministic layers and the paid layers are tiny at development volumes: a single $10 top-up covers roughly 200 Sonnet planning calls or 4,000 Haiku calls, while the genuinely expensive items (generation, GPU hosting, production video analysis on a non-training tier) are optional or can be deferred until there are paying users. The real exposure is not money but data and licensing: the free Gemini route trains on content and is the only free video-capable model, so it works only for synthetic or licensed clips, and several tempting free tiers are commercially unusable.

The credit landscape rewards incorporation and punishes waiting on Anthropic or OpenAI. The only large, bootstrap-reachable credit is Cloudflare's $10k, and it pays for storage and edge inference, not for the models that drive quality, so the stack's quality-critical spend (Gemini, Sonnet, TwelveLabs) will be out of pocket regardless. Because India availability is unconfirmed for every vendor, the first hour of setup should be a signup test of the dozen no-card accounts, recording which ones refuse Indian numbers, cards or regions; that result changes more of this plan than any pricing figure.
