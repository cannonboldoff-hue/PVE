# Layer specialists under Gemini, Sonnet and FFmpeg

The highest-fidelity stack for the Reel editor is a layered hybrid, not a single best model. Frame-exact specialists produce the facts (cut list, OCR boxes and timing, camera-motion vectors, beats, word times), Gemini Flash describes meaning per shot from that cut list, Claude Sonnet 5.5 (through your existing OpenRouter path) plans and revises against a closed, schema-validated template, and FFmpeg plus libass renders, with one browser-based overlay layer (HyperFrames, not Remotion) added for kinetic captions and punchy transitions. The decisive evidence is that general VLMs mislocate transitions by roughly 2-4 s on a public benchmark while TransNetV2/AutoShot-class detectors stay near 1.8 s and are far cheaper ([TransVLM](https://chence17.github.io/TransVLM/)), so VLM timestamps should never define the blueprint. For MCP, almost nothing belongs on the runtime path: migrate the `mcp-video` pin to its renamed successor **Kinocut** (Apache-2.0) and call it as a Python library, and keep community MCPs for Claude Code development only. The licensing traps that matter most for a commercial SaaS are Remotion's company licence (**$100/month minimum, $0.01/render**) once the company has four or more people ([Remotion FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)), AGPL in Ultralytics YOLO and Essentia, and a long list of non-commercial model weights (CrisperWhisper, MatAnyone, RMBG-2.0, DOVER, GIMM-VFI, XTTS v2). My arithmetic puts a deterministic reel at about **$0.15-0.30 all-in** (about $0.15-0.20 of it LLM and vision API spend) and a reel with two generated 4 s fills at roughly **$1-3.5**; none of this was run end to end, and the adoption order below starts with a 10-reel hand-labelled evaluation because every accuracy claim in the sources is either vendor-run, from a different domain, or both.

Labelling convention: "Sourced" marks findings the research notes cite to a URL; "Inference" marks my synthesis or arithmetic. Prices and model names in this period move monthly and several aggregator pages disagree, so verify model IDs on the OpenRouter page before wiring them in.

## Specialists find the facts, VLMs supply the meaning

**Sourced.** Gemini is the strongest general video model and the only major closed API with native video input: Video-MME-v2 names Gemini-3-Pro best with a "substantial gap" to humans ([arXiv 2604.05015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05015)), while Claude and OpenAI document image input only ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision), [OpenAI images guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)). Video-MME itself is near saturation at about 87-90% across aggregators, so it no longer separates top models. The timing evidence is the important part. On the TransVLM public split, boundary frame-F1 / absolute error was TransNetV2 0.528 / 1.87 s, AutoShot 0.532 / 1.78 s, PySceneDetect Adaptive 0.457 / 1.93 s, Gemini 2.5 Pro 0.401 / 3.62 s, Gemini 3 Pro 0.393 / 2.18 s and Qwen3-VL-8B 0.184 / 34.4 s; Gemini only wins on synthetic gradual transitions (0.57-0.69 vs 0.06-0.10 for the detectors). The numbers are the benchmark authors' own ([TransVLM](https://chence17.github.io/TransVLM/)), and an independent re-evaluation found TransNetV2 holds up better than AutoShot on harder shot changes ([Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03575)). Gemini's default is 1 fps sampling at roughly 300 tokens per second, so a 20-30 s reel is about 6-9K input tokens ([Gemini video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)), and developers report invented or out-of-range timestamps ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-2-5-pro-severe-timestamp-timecode-jumping-issues-in-video-transcription-need-workarounds/87242)). Camera motion is weak everywhere: CameraBench found VLMs miss precise geometric trajectories while structure-from-motion misses semantic moves, and a Qwen2.5-VL fine-tune on about 1,400 videos matched MegaSAM ([CameraBench](https://linzhiqiu.github.io/papers/camerabench/)). For on-screen text, PP-OCRv5 is a roughly 5M-parameter specialist reporting 0.067 edit distance on OmniDocBench versus 0.077 for GOT-OCR ([PaddleOCR 3.0](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05595)), but that is a document benchmark, not video, and per-frame OCR needs a tracker to merge frames into one timed string ([Fora Soft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/ai-for-video-engineering/articles-ai/paddleocr-text-detection-video), vendor content).

**Inference.** Treat the cut list as ground truth and give it to the VLM: detect cuts with TransNetV2 or AutoShot, snap to frames with ffprobe, then ask Gemini to describe shot N between two known times, which removes the timestamp-drift failure by construction. Use PySceneDetect (BSD-3, v0.7.2 October 2026, actively maintained) as the fallback detector because TransNetV2's repo has had no push since December 2023 ([PySceneDetect](https://github.com/Breakthrough/PySceneDetect), [TransNetV2 repo](https://api.github.com/repos/soCzech/TransNetV2)); the weights still work. For gradual transitions (dissolve, glitch, whip), pass the surrounding 1-2 s window to the VLM rather than trusting either detector. Compute zoom, pan and shake with OpenCV homography or optical flow and let the VLM break ties; treat "dolly vs zoom" as the known confusion. Take direction of motion from object-track centroids and bounding-box scale change, not from language models.

**Reading the table.** Gemini 3.8 Flash costs $0.75/$3.75 per million tokens until 2026-12-31 and $1.50/$7.50 after; Argon is $2/$10 until 2026-11-30 and $4/$20 after ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Argon's price doubles in under four weeks, which favours Flash as the default. Gemini calls should go direct to Google: whether OpenRouter passes video parts through is contested (one OpenRouter page lists text and image only for Qwen3.7 Plus, another page claims video) ([OpenRouter Qwen3.7 Plus](https://openrouter.ai/models/qwen/qwen3.7-plus-20260602)), and only a GLM row on OpenRouter explicitly lists video input ([OpenRouter models](https://openrouter.ai/models?fmt=table&order=top-weekly)). Qwen3-VL (Apache-2.0) is the best open-weight base with explicit textual timestamp alignment ([Qwen3-VL report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21631)), and a single 24 GB GPU hosts the 8B (an inference), but it trails Gemini in aggregate. TwelveLabs earns a role through `segment_definitions` structured output with `start_time`, `end_time` and typed metadata, the closest off-the-shelf shot table ([TwelveLabs release notes](https://docs.twelvelabs.io/v1.3/docs/get-started/release-notes)); I found no independent benchmark of it against Gemini and no camera-motion accuracy evidence, so run it as an A/B arm rather than the default. The TwelveLabs billing change matters to you as an existing customer: plans (Free 3 GB, Plus $29/50 GB, Pro $99/200 GB) took effect 2026-10-15, with existing customers migrating from 2027-01-04, and per-unit workload rates come only from third-party trackers ([TwelveLabs pricing](https://www.twelvelabs.io/pricing), [usagepricing.com](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/twelve-labs-2026-10-15-subscription-plans-replace-payg)). Marengo 3.5 produces embeddings only; managed search still needs Marengo 3.0.

## Planning, matching and verification form one bounded loop

**Sourced.** Anthropic's price list gives Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10 per million tokens with 1M context, Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50 (rising above 100K-token prompts), Opus 5.5 $4/$20, batch at half price and a Sonnet 5.5 cache-read rate of $0.10 per million ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Public tool-calling leaderboards disagree on the winner: LLM Stats has GPT-6 Astra 36.0, Sonnet 5.5 35.9 and Gemini 4 Carbon 35.4 in a statistical tie ([LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/leaderboards/best-ai-for-tool-calling)), while BenchLM ranks Qwen3.7 Plus first at 72 and GLM-5.1 as the top open-weight model ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/best/tool-use)). Neither isolates JSON validity. For embeddings, Gemini Embedding 2 handles video up to 120 s and 32 frames but ignores the clip's audio ([Gemini embeddings docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/embeddings)), and no independent benchmark compares Marengo 3.5, Gemini Embedding 2, Voyage multimodal-3.5, SigLIP 2, InternVideo2 and VideoPrism on short-clip retrieval.

**Inference.** Sonnet 5.5 is the planner and chat agent because it is a top-three tool caller at a mid price and handles images (keyframe grids) natively, with Haiku 5.5 as a first attempt that escalates on schema failure. Embeddings give recall and a VLM re-rank gives precision, which matches the earlier landscape report's pipeline of tags as hard filters, ANN search, then a VLM rerank of the top 10-20 against the full slot spec (the earlier competitor-landscape research, which cites [ReCLIP](https://nlp.cs.berkeley.edu/pubs/Subramanian-Merrill-Darrell-Gardner-Singh-Rohrbach_2022_ReCLIP_paper.pdf) for embeddings being weak on direction, negation and action binding). Build a 50-query, 200-clip retrieval set from real user footage before committing to Marengo or Gemini Embedding 2, since accuracy between them is unproven for this task.

**Verification, sourced.** No paper does reference-video template extraction with a closed verification loop; the nearest are EditDuet and AVE-Agent. EditDuet's Editor-Critic pair had an 8.2% failure rate with failures such as hallucinated functions and out-of-bounds trims, and its GPT-4o judge on keyframe grids agreed with the majority human vote 80.6% of the time versus 78.7% human-human ([EditDuet](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10761v1)). AVE-Agent keeps the highest-scoring candidate so a later unstable attempt cannot overwrite a better one and picks the cheapest fix (remix, regenerate, replan) ([AVE-Compass](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24821v1)). Judge bias is real: one 2026 benchmark found slot order shifts win rates by about 17.7 points and repeat runs agree only 83-87% (a search-snippet-derived figure; the PDF body was not confirmed) ([MEDit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25300)), and pooling weak video-LM judges does not reliably help ([arXiv 2503.05977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05977v1)). Beat-alignment metrics come from music-to-dance work with no standard tolerance, and the literature uses windows near 100 ms ([Video Echoed in Music](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09585)). The best agent on AgenticVBench resolves only about 27-30% of real editing tasks, mostly failing quality tests rather than tool calls ([Timeline-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35143)).

**Verification, inference.** Layer the checks from free and exact to costly and fuzzy and stop at the first failure. Layer 0 lints the plan (clip IDs exist, trims in bounds, text inside a configurable safe zone, cuts snapped to beats). Layer 1 is the ffprobe/FFmpeg gate (resolution, fps, `freezedetect`, `blackdetect`, `silencedetect`, loudness). Layer 2 compares structure to the blueprint with no model: boundary F1 at ±2 and ±5 frames, shot-length curve distance, cut-hit and beat-coverage rates scored relative to the reference, OCR string match and box IoU against the planned text layer, per-shot Lab statistics and motion-energy correlation. Layer 3 uses SigLIP 2 or DreamSim only for clip-to-slot semantic fit, never for similarity to the reference, because the user's content should differ from the reference's. Layer 4 is a Gemini Flash pairwise judge on keyframe grids plus blueprint JSON, both orders swapped, order-inconsistent verdicts marked uncertain. Every finding becomes a typed JSON patch; keep the argmax candidate, cap at two or three rounds and stop when the gain is below epsilon. The round cap and Layer 2 tolerances are my recommendations; no source validates an iteration count. Cross-family judging (Gemini judging, Sonnet patching) is my design choice with no evidence for or against it in the notes.

## Audio, captions and rendering: FFmpeg as base, one browser layer for the last mile

**Sourced, audio.** Beat This! is MIT, needs no DBN post-processing and reports beat/downbeat F1 of 97.5/95.3 on Ballroom and 62.7 on SMC, with weaker continuity scores ([arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21658), [repo](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this)). WhisperX is BSD-2-Clause with wav2vec2 forced alignment, and plain Whisper timestamps "can be inaccurate by several seconds" ([WhisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX)). CrisperWhisper claims 29.6 ms word-boundary error against WhisperX's 64.8 ms on TIMIT, but its weights are non-commercial ([CrisperWhisper](https://github.com/nyrahealth/CrisperWhisper)). python-audio-separator is MIT and lists BS-RoFormer vocals at 12.9 SDR versus 10.8 for htdemucs_ft ([audio-separator](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator)). ShazamIO is MIT but a reverse-engineered scrape of a private API that sometimes returns confident wrong matches ([ShazamIO](https://github.com/shazamio/ShazamIO)). Scribe v2 costs $0.22 per audio hour ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). Loudness guidance is third-party consensus of -14 LUFS and -1 dBTP ([DEV](https://dev.to/arthurvalle1_a2586dd2b4bc/mastering-for-reels-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts-the-lufs-14-standard-2ck5)).

**Inference, audio.** Separate once and early with a RoFormer vocal model, run beats on the instrumental stem, ASR plus forced alignment on the vocal stem, and snap cuts to the nearest beat within about ±40 ms. A 30-60 ms word-timing error is below one frame at 30 fps (33 ms), so WhisperX-grade timing is enough for captions and CrisperWhisper's gap is likely invisible. If the reference captions are burned in rather than spoken, OCR timing from frames is the ground truth, so keep both paths. For music, report "identified track" via ShazamIO for the MVP with AudD or ACRCloud as budgeted fallbacks, and suggest licensed alternatives by tempo, key and energy filtering followed by CLAP similarity (CLAP and MERT licences were not verified). Never carry the reference's audio into the output.

**Sourced, rendering.** HyperFrames (HeyGen, Apache-2.0, 78.8k stars, pushed 2026-11-04) renders HTML/CSS/GSAP compositions frame by frame in headless Chrome and encodes with FFmpeg, ships caption and WebGL shader-transition blocks, requires Node 22+, and its own README concedes Remotion Lambda is the more mature cloud renderer ([HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)). Kinocut (formerly mcp-video, Apache-2.0, 243 stars, v1.16.5 on 2026-10-27, weekly releases since August) wraps FFmpeg plus 18 Hyperframes tools and offers preflight, `video_quality_check` and hashed "Video Receipts", but its transitions are limited to glitch, morph and pixelate, its README does not list speed ramps or zoom/pan, and its grading tools are still-image only ([Kinocut](https://github.com/KyaniteLabs/kinocut), [PyPI mcp-video](https://pypi.org/project/mcp-video/)). FFmpeg's real limits are documented: `zoompan` steps in whole pixels and jitters unless the input is supersampled first ([Bannerbear](https://www.bannerbear.com/blog/how-to-do-a-ken-burns-style-effect-with-ffmpeg)), `xfade` fails with variable or mismatched frame rates, and exotic GL-style transitions need either a patched FFmpeg or slow expression mode ([xfade-easing](https://github.com/scriptituk/xfade-easing)). pycaps (MIT, alpha, 230 stars) needs Chromium and is not on PyPI ([pycaps](https://github.com/francozanardi/pycaps)). OpenTimelineIO (Apache-2.0) carries cuts, transitions and markers but not text animation or grade, and EDL/FCP7 export drops transitions or tracks ([OTIO adapters](https://opentimelineio.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/adapters.html)).

**Inference, rendering.** Keep FFmpeg as cutter, compositor, grader and final encoder, with libass for caption presets (per-word `\k` timing, outline, shadow, `\t` scale pops). Normalise every clip to 1080x1920, constant 30 fps, `setpts=PTS-STARTPTS` before any `xfade`. Add HyperFrames only to render transparent overlays (kinetic captions, spring pops, shader or whip transitions), then composite in FFmpeg; that hybrid also sidesteps the cost of many video layers inside Chrome. Transparent-overlay export and the HyperFrames CLI on Windows are unconfirmed, so test them first. Because Kinocut is guardrails rather than template motion, keep it as the agent-facing interface and verification layer and put your own render functions (speed ramp, supersampled zoom, LUT, caption preset) behind the same interface. About 90% of fidelity should come from timing, framing, speed ramp, grade and caption style, which FFmpeg plus ASS can deliver; this "90%" is my estimate, not a measurement.

**Deterministic helpers (sourced picks, inferred recipes).** For grade matching, fit a mapping in Lab/OKLab between reference and target frames, bake it to a `.cube` and apply with `lut3d`; colour-science is BSD-3 ([colour-science](https://github.com/colour-science/colour)), and no neural preset with a commercial licence was found. For reframing, Google AutoFlip support ended 2023-03-01 ([MediaPipe guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/guide)), so detect subjects with MediaPipe (Apache-2.0) or SAM 2 (Apache-2.0, [SAM 2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2)), smooth with a deadzone plus Kalman/EMA filter, and emit crop keyframes into the template. For stabilisation, FFmpeg vidstab two-pass; for slow motion, Practical-RIFE (MIT, [repo](https://github.com/hzwer/Practical-RIFE)); for upscaling, FlashVSR (Apache-2.0, about 41 s for a 10 s 720p clip to 1080p per ComfyUI docs, [FlashVSR](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR), [ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/video-upscale.md)), gated to run only when the source's shorter side is below target. Cutouts: BiRefNet (MIT, [repo](https://github.com/zhengpeng7/BiRefNet)).

**Template schema (inference).** No public standard captures a Reel template, and no openly licensed Reel-template dataset exists. Use a closed, enumerated JSON schema: an OTIO-style structure spine (tracks, slots with role tags, constraints), keyframe tracks with easing, a transitions vocabulary mapped to xfade and HyperFrames equivalents, text items with preset ids and word-timing source, grade as LUT plus parameters, audio with beat grid, and per-field extraction confidence for the verifier. Closed presets make LLM output schema-validatable and patchable.

## MCP: nothing is required at runtime, and the developer toolbox is separate

**Sourced.** `mcp-video` on PyPI is now version 1.6.20, "Compatibility installer for Kinocut, formerly mcp-video", pinning `kinocut==1.16.5`; new users are told to `pip install kinocut` ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mcp-video/)). Kinocut exposes 205 MCP tools only with `KINOCUT_TOOLS=all` (default is a curated 20-tool core), runs over stdio (`uvx --from kinocut kino --mcp`), also offers a Python `Client`, calls itself a "publication checkpoint" with future releases "TBD", and states its controls are "not a network sandbox" ([Kinocut](https://github.com/KyaniteLabs/kinocut)). Remotion shut down its hosted docs MCP on 2026-10-26, citing lagging data, token cost and unreliable agent invocation, and replaced it with Agent Skills ([Remotion docs](https://remotion.dev/docs/ai/mcp)). ElevenLabs archived its local MCP in favour of a hosted OAuth server ([ElevenLabs MCP repo](https://github.com/elevenlabs/elevenlabs-mcp)). TwelveLabs offers an official hosted OAuth MCP whose tool list and licence are not published ([TwelveLabs docs](https://docs.twelvelabs.io/docs/advanced/model-context-protocol)). Replicate's official server is listed in the MCP Registry, which stores metadata only and is not a code audit ([Replicate changelog](https://replicate.com/changelog/2026-02-10-mcp-server-auto-discovery)); Hugging Face's hosted MCP can add community Spaces as tools, which executes third-party code ([HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/en/agents-mcp)). Documented security incidents include a malicious Postmark-impersonating npm package, tool-poisoning attacks, and an NVD-listed injection in the Blender MCP ([dev.to security review](https://dev.to/luckypipewrench/the-state-of-mcp-security-2026-incidents-attack-patterns-and-defense-coverage-2h45), [CVE-2026-10661](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/cve-2026-10661)). Playwright MCP and Chrome DevTools MCP are the de facto UI-testing and debugging servers, and Context7 serves version-specific library docs; star counts come from conflicting directories ([devtk.ai](https://devtk.ai/en/directory/mcp-servers/playwright/)).

**Inference, runtime.** Put no MCP server on the render or ingest path of the FastAPI backend. Call Kinocut's Python `Client` (pinned, telemetry off, FFmpeg path set) from the render service, and call Gemini, OpenRouter, TwelveLabs, ElevenLabs and fal through their SDKs; MCP adds token overhead and a prompt-injection surface, and reference reels and user clips deliver untrusted text through captions, OCR, transcripts, filenames and ID3/EXIF metadata. If the chat reviser should drive tools through MCP, expose Kinocut over stdio with the default 20-tool core or `search_tools`, give each pipeline step its own allow-list, and require confirmation for destructive or outbound actions. Check that the `kinocut` import path and API match your current `mcp-video` usage before migrating, and expect churn from weekly 1.x releases.

**Inference, developer workflow in Claude Code.** Adopt Context7 for FFmpeg, FastAPI and React docs, Playwright MCP for end-to-end flows (upload, chat revise, preview), Chrome DevTools MCP for preview performance (you already have a Chrome bridge connected, so there is overlap), and Replicate's official and Hugging Face's hosted MCPs for quick model trials, with Hugging Face dynamic Spaces discovery switched off. Install Remotion Agent Skills or HyperFrames skills (`npx remotion skills add`) rather than a docs MCP. Figma and Canva MCP tools are reference-only: from the tool schemas of the connected servers (no public URL), Canva's `create-video` generates new AI video from a spec, and Figma's `get_motion_context` exposes keyframe and easing data, but neither analyses an existing Reel into a template. Treat the DaVinci Resolve MCP (MIT, desktop GUI required, [repo](https://github.com/samuelgursky/davinci-resolve-mcp)), VectCutAPI for CapCut draft export ([repo](https://github.com/sun-guannan/VectCutAPI)) and Pexels servers as optional; avoid social downloaders and publishers, unofficial Runway/Luma/Kling wrappers, Adobe UXP bridges that execute arbitrary JavaScript, the archived local ElevenLabs server, and Blender MCP outside an isolated dev machine.

## Licensing traps for a commercial SaaS

**Sourced, with the legal readings marked as inference.** The traps cluster in four groups. The first is the company licence: Remotion is free for individuals, teams of up to three, nonprofits and evaluators, headcount counts the whole company, Automators pricing is $0.01 per successful render with a $100/month minimum, Creators is $25/month per coder (including via agentic tools), users may not upload their own Remotion projects for rendering, and Remotion 5.0 will make render telemetry mandatory for Automators ([Remotion FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)). This first-party FAQ supersedes the earlier note's secondary-only figures and contradicts a pkgpulse article's different tiers. The second is copyleft: Ultralytics YOLO is AGPL-3.0 ([Ultralytics](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics)); Essentia is AGPL-3.0 and Video2X is AGPL-3.0; Gyroflow, Etro and Robust Video Matting are GPL-3.0; FFmpeg's vidstab (GPL) matters only if you distribute an FFmpeg binary built with it, which is a legal inference. The third is non-commercial or restricted weights: CrisperWhisper weights ([repo](https://github.com/nyrahealth/CrisperWhisper)), MatAnyone's S-Lab licence ([repo](https://github.com/pq-yang/MatAnyone)), RMBG-2.0 CC BY-NC 4.0 ([BRIA](https://github.com/Bria-AI/RMBG-2.0)), DOVER and Q-Align's S-Lab licence ([DOVER](https://github.com/VQAssessment/DOVER)), GIMM-VFI ([repo](https://github.com/GSeanCDAT/GIMM-VFI)), XTTS v2 and F5-TTS official weights ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/xtts-coqui-commercial-license)), and InsightFace pretrained models ([InsightFace](https://www.insightface.ai/solutions/face-recognition-licensing)). The fourth is territory and revenue clauses: MiniMax H3 weights reportedly exclude the US, EU, UK and South Korea and require authorisation above $20M revenue ([NYU Shanghai summary](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/minimax-ships-h3-weights-with-the-us-and-eu-excluded/)); HunyuanVideo 1.5 does not apply in the EU, UK or South Korea ([license via GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-1.5)); LTX-2.3 weights sit under a community licence with a reported $10M ARR threshold while the repo code is Apache-2.0 ([LTX-2](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2)); and SAM 3's custom licence is royalty-free but silent on commercial use, with indemnity, termination and unilateral-change clauses ([SAM 3](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3)), so SAM 2 (Apache-2.0) is the zero-risk choice.

**Platform and data traps.** Server-side fetching of Reel links is a terms problem on all three platforms: Instagram oEmbed returns embed HTML only and bars use for analytics, extraction or persistence ([Meta oEmbed](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/oembed)), and YouTube's API policy prohibits downloading, caching, storing or separating audio and video ([YouTube policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)). Social-media MCP downloaders rely on scraper APIs and amplify this. Accept uploaded files with a rights attestation. Gemini Omni video edit and extend is unavailable in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK for uploaded video ([Gemini Omni docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)). Use paid API tiers only, because paid Gemini does not train on your data while OpenAI keeps abuse logs up to 30 days ([Gemini ZDR](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/zdr.md.txt), [OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)), and get a written DPA from TwelveLabs because its privacy-policy language ("improve services") conflicts with its terms ([TwelveLabs privacy](https://twelvelabs.io/privacy)). Face identity matching remains the highest-liability feature (BIPA, Texas CUBI, GDPR), so detect and count faces only. Music: ShazamIO is a scrape of a private API (MVP only), Jamendo's free tier is non-commercial, and Epidemic Sound's production price is unpublished ([Epidemic Sound](https://www.epidemicsound.com/business/developers/)).

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that the stack's quality ceiling is set by what you refuse to ask models to do. Every source that measured timing shows VLMs 2-4 s adrift on boundaries, and judge studies show 83-87% repeat agreement with large order effects, so a design that lets Gemini define cut times or lets a single judge decide pass or fail inherits those errors, while a design that hands Gemini a detector's cut list, scores structure with no-model metrics first, and converts findings into bounded patches against a closed schema turns the same models into reliable components. Because the renderer owns the text layer, plan-versus-render checks are nearly exact; only style fields need the reference, which shrinks the fuzzy part of verification to a few judge calls.

The second takeaway is that the commercial risk sits in a few small decisions, not in model choice: one Remotion headcount threshold, one AGPL YOLO import, one non-commercial checkpoint or matting model, and one link-fetch feature can each outweigh any quality gain. Nothing in this report has been run, and no published benchmark covers reference-to-template recreation, so the 10-reel evaluation and the gold set of reference and render pairs are not a validation step to add later; they are the instrument that decides whether Gemini Flash, Argon, Pegasus or Qwen3-VL earns each blueprint field.
