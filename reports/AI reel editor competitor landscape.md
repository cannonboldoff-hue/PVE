# Building a Reel editor that remembers: thin white space, real risks

The full loop you describe (reference Reel, ShotSpec blueprint, persistent library match, "missing shot" instructions, edit, verify, chat feedback) is not shipped as a single product that the research could find, but every individual piece exists and the closest competitor, invideo's agentic editor, already covers about half of it. The white space is real but narrow: a persistent, cross-project personal footage index queried against a reference-derived shot spec, with explicit exact/similar/missing classification and per-shot recording instructions. That position rests on absence of evidence, not on testing, because no competitor product was used hands-on. The technology is buildable today for cents per Reel (about **$0.05-0.15** deterministic, **$1-3** with generative fill, both order-of-magnitude estimates), so the binding risks are commercial and legal: unproven library hit rate, weak AI-app retention (**21.1%** annual), platform copycats (CapCut, Instagram Edits, invideo), and a legal minefield around ingesting third-party Reel links and recognising faces. The recommendation is to build, but only after a two-to-three-week offline test of library coverage and a concierge test of "record these shots" behaviour, with reference input limited to user-supplied files, no face identity matching, and deterministic editing first with generative fill deferred. Weak evidence is flagged throughout; the notes themselves contain no Reddit or App Store user quotes, so demand for this exact workflow is inferred, not observed.

## No competitor closes the loop, but invideo covers half of it

**invideo's agentic editor is the main threat.** Its "Match an edit using reference videos" feature studies an uploaded or YouTube-linked reference and recreates pacing, transitions, text animation and sound design on your footage, with timestamp-level control and a fully editable timeline ([invideo reference page](https://invideo.io/make/style-and-motion-reference/)). Its agents "watched the footage, not just the transcript," include a Search agent that finds "people, actions, objects, camera angles," and take plain-language revision requests ([invideo agent page](https://invideo.io/make/agentic-video-editor/)). Its own FAQ admits matching works best when your footage supports the reference, noting that a multi-location, complex-camera reference may not translate to one static recording ([invideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/can-an-ai-editing-agent-match-the-pacing-and-style-of-a-reference-video/)). That sentence describes your missing-shot problem exactly, which validates the pain but also shows invideo knows about it. What the pages do not show is a cross-project library, a missing-shot request flow or a ShotSpec output; these are inferences from page text. Neither page gives a launch date, and pricing is third-party and conflicting (Plus roughly $17-28/month, agent usage credit-metered) ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-video-editing-saas/invideo-ai-edit/)). Test it on your own reels before spending more.

**The blueprint half exists in small startups.** ViralClip breaks a pasted TikTok/Reel/Short into hook, pacing, shot list and captions and rebuilds it around your product, but it generates footage with AI rather than matching your real clips; its only source is a directory listing dated 2026-08-19, and its own site returned HTTP 410 when fetched ([SimilarLabs listing](https://similarlabs.com/zh/p/viralclip-viral-video-cloner-blueprint)). Virash (Product Hunt, about March 2026) and CloneViral (reported $29/$89 per month, October 2025) clone viral formats, and Vireel drew 552 Product Hunt upvotes in August 2025, which is now more than 12 months old ([Product Hunt maker profile](https://www.producthunt.com/@mangata), [trendingaitools](https://www.trendingaitools.com/?p=35487), [hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/cracked-ai/launches/vireel)). Mosaic (YC W25) said reference style transfer is planned and that retrieval over roughly 1 TB of footage is not yet supported ([Launch HN](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/45980760)). Most "clone a viral reel" Product Hunt launches have negligible traction (Kiki 0 upvotes), so demand exists but no breakout winner has emerged.

**Distribution incumbents solve slot-filling, not extraction.** CapCut templates sync a user's clips to a creator-authored template's timing and music ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/help/latest-capcut-editing-trends)). Instagram's own help confirms templates reuse the original Reel's music and timing while you swap in clips ([Instagram help](https://help.instagram.com/android-app/610485296790527)); Edits also offers Storyboards, a teleprompter and, since June 2026, an AI assistant for ideas and analytics ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/amp/en/technology/metas-edit-app-to-receive-ai-assistant-desktop-version-and-new-creator-tools-enn26061203419)). The notes could not confirm that any template system auto-assigns clips by content similarity. Library search products are separate: Google Photos highlight videos pick shots from typed place/person/date queries ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/photos/photo-to-video-remix-create-tab)), and Clipto ($15M at $250M post-money, August 2026, on-device indexing with an MCP server) and Shade ($14M, April 2026) index personal or creative-team libraries but lean professional ([VCA Online](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2026083103/clipto-raises-15m-to-build-the-memory-layer-for-the-ai-era/), [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/shade-raises-14m-for-ai-powered-video-search-and-cloud-storage-platform)). Guided recording exists only as shot-list checklists (SimplistUGC, $2.99/month) and teleprompters ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/simplistugc/id6759472145)). Generative tools (Runway, Higgsfield, Kling) offer the opposite answer to a missing shot: synthesize it instead of asking the user to film it.

| Capability | Closest existing | Gap vs your idea |
|---|---|---|
| Reference to edit | invideo, ViralClip, CloneViral | No persistent library; ViralClip generates rather than matches |
| Library search | Clipto, Shade, Google Photos, Premiere, Resolve 21 | Not tied to reference-driven editing |
| Missing-shot guidance | SimplistUGC | Checklist only; no diff against a library |
| Chat edits | Kapwing Kai, Captions AI Edit, Descript | Operate on a timeline, not on a gap-analysed plan |

## The white space is a combination, and each piece is cheap to copy

The defensible claim is the combination: an index that persists across projects, a gap analysis against a blueprint, and per-shot recording instructions. Each piece is individually thin, and the notes' own inference is that invideo or CapCut could add a missing-shots prompt cheaply, while Google Photos and Apple already own the personal library and Instagram owns the reference-Reel context and the user's Reels history (weekly personalised clip ideas shipped January 2026) ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/edits-gets-ig-links-weekly-ideas-and-new-video-effects-features/809644/)). An APK teardown of CapCut 19.9.0 (2026-10-23) infers an "EditPilot" LLM copilot but found no strings for reference shot lists or library search ([Android Sage](https://www.androidsage.com/2026/10/23/capcut-19-9-0-apk/)); that is inference from code, not an announcement. A moat from the index itself is weak unless it contains user-confirmed metadata (labels, favourites, brand preferences): raw embeddings are recomputable by anyone from the same files, and Clipto's MCP server shows interoperability can erode lock-in.

**Three assumptions carry the business, and none has an empirical test in the notes.** First, library hit rate: personal libraries skew to family, travel, pets and food, so a slot like "wide establishing shot, subject walking left to right, golden hour" will often have no strong match. Premiere's own search returned over 1,000 results for "drone" and community reports describe weak results for generic queries ([Adobe Learn](https://www.adobe.com/learn/premiere-pro/web/media-intelligence-search-ai)); Google paused Ask Photos in 2025 over "latency, quality and UX" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/06/27/google-resumes-rollout-of-ai-based-ask-photos-image-search)). The notes found no published fill-rate number for any system, so "no good match" must be a first-class output state. Second, cold start: a new user has an empty library, so day-one value must come from the record-these-shots path. Third, willingness to record: nothing shows users will film eight precisely specified shots, and the demand evidence is indirect (a Descript feature request for B-roll from your own clips, and a note that most B-roll tools use stock or generated footage) ([Descript feedback](https://feedback.descript.com/features/p/ai-b-roll-suggestion)).

**The economics push toward a narrow buyer and per-Reel pricing.** The generic editing price anchor is $0 (CapCut free core, Edits free, with Mosseri floating paid AI features later). AI subscription apps show annual retention of **21.1%** versus 30.7% for non-AI, monthly retention 6.1% versus 9.5%, a 4.2% median refund rate, and median realized LTV of **$18.92** monthly / **$30.16** annual ([TechCrunch on RevenueCat](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)). The most consistent incumbent complaint is billing and credit opacity: Opus Clip Trustpilot reviews cite unrefunded credits and deleted projects, and Captions drew a roughly 1.5/5 score on one regional snapshot ([Trustpilot Opus](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/opus.pro?page=4), [Trustpilot Captions](https://se.trustpilot.com/review/captions.ai)). A transparent per-Reel or capped monthly price is therefore a plausible differentiator. The highest-intent buyers are likely businesses posting often on recurring subjects (restaurants, real estate, e-commerce, agencies), but no segment-level willingness-to-pay data was found; this is inference. Funding does validate the memory thesis (Clipto, Shade, OpusSearch), yet those players chose professionals with large archives first, which suggests consumer personal-library search was not an easy wedge. Market-size figures are unusable: AI video editing/generation estimates range from $0.71B to $4.67B for 2026 ([MEAN CEO](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11528)), and CapCut MAU claims range from about 500M to 800M with no primary source.

## Every component is purchasable, so the cost is reliability, not inference

**Reference analysis: use a hybrid, not one model.** Gemini takes native video plus audio at about 300 tokens per second at default resolution (about 100 at low), with structured output, YouTube URLs and custom frame rates ([Gemini video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)). Analysing a 15-30 second reel costs roughly **$0.01-0.05** on Gemini Flash-class models (3.8 Flash at $0.75/$3.75 per million tokens, rising to $1.50/$7.50 after 2026-12-31) ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)); TwelveLabs Pegasus 1.6 is about **$0.03-0.04** on overage-equivalent rates, and its `segment_definitions` structured output with `start_time`, `end_time` and typed metadata is the closest off-the-shelf shot table ([TwelveLabs release notes](https://docs.twelvelabs.io/v1.3/docs/get-started/release-notes)). The weakness is precision: developers report invented or out-of-range Gemini timestamps, and a preprint (not independently checked) reports omitted events under dense temporal load ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-2-5-pro-severe-timestamp-timecode-jumping-issues-in-video-transcription-need-workarounds/87242), [arXiv 2608.06361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06361)). Camera movement is the weakest field everywhere: CameraBench found VLMs miss precise geometric trajectories while structure-from-motion misses semantic moves, and fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL on about 1,400 videos helped ([CameraBench](https://linzhiqiu.github.io/papers/camerabench/)). No evidence was found of TwelveLabs or Gemini camera-motion accuracy. The notes' design inference is therefore: TransNetV2 (MIT) or PySceneDetect (BSD-3, v0.7.2 October 2026) for cut boundaries snapped via ffprobe, OpenCV optical-flow or homography for pan/tilt/zoom, object tracks for direction of motion, a VLM per shot clip for shot size, action, text and style, and librosa or Beat This (MIT) for beats ([TransNetV2](https://github.com/soCzech/TransNetV2), [PySceneDetect](https://github.com/Breakthrough/PySceneDetect), [Beat This](https://api.github.com/repos/CPJKU/beat_this)). Claude accepts images only per the docs read, so a frames-only path costs far more tokens (about 62-94K for a 20-30 s reel versus about 6-9K for Gemini) ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)).

**Library index: embeddings plus structured tags plus rerank.** Dense embeddings handle scene and mood but are weak on direction, negation and action binding ([ReCLIP paper](https://nlp.cs.berkeley.edu/pubs/Subramanian-Merrill-Darrell-Gardner-Singh-Rohrbach_2022_ReCLIP_paper.pdf)). The supportable pipeline is shot segmentation, per-shot embedding, VLM-extracted tags stored as columns (shot size, motion, subject count, location, lighting), hard filtering on tags, then ANN search and a VLM rerank of the top 10-20 against the full slot spec. Options: Gemini Embedding 2 (video up to 120 s, max 32 frames, ignores the video's audio, about $0.00079 per frame) or TwelveLabs Marengo 3.5 (512 dims default, composed queries, vendor-reported 67.38 vs 62.35 for Gemini, unreproduced) ([Gemini embeddings docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/embeddings), [Marengo 3.5](https://www.twelvelabs.io/blog/marengo-3.5)). Marengo 3.5 emits embeddings only, so managed search still needs Marengo 3.0. TwelveLabs moved to subscription plans on 2026-10-15 (Free 3 GB, Plus $29 / 50 GB, Pro $99 / 200 GB) and per-unit workload rates are published only by third-party trackers ([TwelveLabs pricing](https://www.twelvelabs.io/pricing)); its privacy policy language ("improve services") conflicts with its terms, so get a written DPA. Embedding cost scales with how finely you segment: roughly **$0.76-2.84 per hour** of footage at 32-120 s chunks (half with batch), but about **$5.70 per hour** at 4-second segments with 8 frames each, which is the dominant infrastructure cost ([notes' arithmetic from Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Vector storage is trivial: 100,000 segments at 512 dimensions is about 200 MB, so pgvector (HNSW, with iterative scan needed for filtered queries) is sufficient and managed minimums ($16-50 per month for turbopuffer or Pinecone) exceed the raw cost ([pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector), [turbopuffer](https://turbopuffer.com/pricing)). Quality prior for a match score: DOVER separates aesthetic and technical scores (check its licence) ([DOVER](https://github.com/VQAssessment/DOVER)). A "94% match" percentage is a design choice; calibrate it on accept/reject logs or show a rank badge.

**Rendering: deterministic and cached.** Render each shot to a normalised intermediate keyed by content hash, then concat, captions and audio in one cheap pass, so a feedback edit invalidates only the changed shots (design inference; the notes found no tested implementation). Remotion Lambda costs about **$0.021 per rendered minute** ([Remotion cost example](https://remotion.dev/docs/lambda/cost-example)) but Remotion is source-available, free only for individuals, nonprofits and for-profits with up to 3 employees; the company licence prices ($0.01 per render, $100/month minimum) are secondary-source only ([Remotion LICENSE](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). Shotstack is 5-20 cents per minute ([Shotstack pricing](https://shotstack.io/pricing/)). Editly is stale (last push May 2025) ([GitHub](https://api.github.com/repos/mifi/editly)); Gyroflow (GPL-3.0) and Essentia (AGPL-3.0) are poor fits for closed SaaS. Plain FFmpeg handles trims, xfade and audio mixing at no licence cost. Open agentic editors such as browser-use/video-use (MIT, 30.3k stars) work from transcripts and an EDL, with a capped self-eval loop, and "never watch the video," so they suit talking-head footage, not silent B-roll Reels ([video-use](https://github.com/browser-use/video-use)). Benchmarks are sobering: the best agent resolves about **27-30%** of real editing tasks, and most failures are quality tests rather than tool errors ([AgenticVBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27705), [Timeline-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35143)). That supports an LLM that emits schema-validated timeline patches on shot IDs, shows the plan before execution (the LAVE pattern), and runs a bounded repair loop ([LAVE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10294v1)).

**Audio, captions and generative fill.** ASR is negligible: ElevenLabs Scribe v2 is $0.22 per hour (about $0.001 per Reel), and faster-whisper or WhisperX are MIT/BSD options ([ElevenLabs API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). TTS for 300 characters is about $0.005-0.024. Music is the real problem. Cheap compliant tracks are Lyria clips ($0.04) or ElevenLabs Music ($0.15/minute); Instagram's Audio API can attach licensed or trending audio at publish time for Business/Creator accounts but cannot preview and cannot beat-sync to the track ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [Instagram Audio API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/content-publishing/audio-api)). Epidemic Sound's API offers a free prototyping tier but its production price is unpublished, and Epidemic is itself suing Meta over Reels Remix, a case still active in 2026 ([Epidemic Sound developers](https://www.epidemicsound.com/business/developers/), [Courthouse News filing](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/reels-remix.pdf)). For generative fill, costs run 10-100 times the deterministic stack: Gemini Omni Flash is about $0.10 per second at 720p, with edit/extend limited to inputs of 10 seconds or less and unavailable in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, and a SynthID watermark on all output ([Gemini Omni docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)). The Veo 3.1 preview endpoints shut down on 2026-10-22, OpenAI's Sora API ended on 2026-09-24, and Veo, Kling and Runway per-second prices are aggregator figures ([Gemini video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-sets-two-stage-sora-shutdown-with-app-closing-april-2026-and-api-following-in-september/)). Plan 2-3 retries per kept shot. Combining these pieces, estimated COGS is **$0.05-0.15 per deterministic Reel** and **$1-3 with 8 seconds of generative footage**, excluding LLM planning calls, storage and egress. Storage of raw footage (tens to hundreds of GB per active user) was never quantified in the notes and may be the largest recurring cost, which argues for on-device indexing with proxy upload.

## Reference ingestion and face recognition are the legal chokepoints

Not legal advice; the notes' legal research hit rate limits, relied heavily on law-firm summaries, and could not confirm the founder's jurisdiction (India is assumed, unverified).

**Do not fetch Reel links server-side.** Instagram's oEmbed returns embed HTML only and bars use of its content for analytics, extraction or persistence ([Meta oEmbed docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/oembed)). YouTube's API policies prohibit downloading, caching or storing audiovisual content without written approval, bar separating audio or video components, and bar scraping ([YouTube API Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)); its ToS also bars circumventing copy restrictions ([YouTube ToS](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)). TikTok's ToS reportedly prohibits automated extraction, but the notes could not reach TikTok's own pages and the secondary source gives conflicting ToS dates ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/tiktok/tiktok-terms-of-service/prohibition-on-scraping-and-data-extraction/)). Meta v. Bright Data (January 2024) offers thin cover: it covered only logged-off scraping contract claims in a district court ([Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-what-does-the-meta-v-bright-data-summary-judgment-ruling-mean-for-web-scraping/)). Competitors that accept YouTube links may do so for the user's own or licensed content, but their actual terms were not retrieved. The safe tiers are a user-supplied file or screen recording with a rights attestation, share-sheet receipt, then embed-only display, with link-paste fetching as the high-risk option. Note the tension: invideo advertises YouTube-link reference input, and so a link-based UX is a feature gap you accept deliberately.

**Copyright risk is lower for structure and higher for audio.** The notes found no case law on whether video pacing, shot order and format are protected expression, so the usual idea/expression reasoning is untested here. Analysis of a user-supplied reference mainly creates a reproduction question, where fair use and EU TDM arguments are fact-specific; the US Copyright Office's pre-publication Part 3 report says dataset compilation "clearly implicates the right of reproduction" and its final status is uncertain ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/us-copyright-office-releases-major-ai-training-report-amid-intensifying-copyright-debate/)). Kadrey v. Meta found fair use because plaintiffs failed to show market harm and the judge said a better record could change the result ([Jackson Walker](https://www.jw.com/news/insights-kadrey-meta-bartz-anthropic-ai-copyright/)). Never carry the reference's audio, text or footage into the output. Music exposure is practical: business Instagram accounts are limited to Meta's Sound Collection or separately licensed tracks, and sources conflict on whether Sound Collection audio can be reused off-Meta ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/use-music-social-media-platforms-what-businesses-and-creators-need-know-about-usage), [Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/instagram-sound-library-rules-for-creators-and-brands)).

**Faces are the highest-liability feature.** BIPA's 2024 amendment caps recovery at one per person ($1,000 negligent, $5,000 intentional) and the Seventh Circuit applied it retroactively in April 2026, which reduces but does not remove class risk ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/04/08/seventh-circuit-limits-potential-damages-under-bipa-holds-2024-amendment-applies-retroactively/)). Google settled a Photos face-grouping BIPA class action for $100M and agreed to opt-in consent and template deletion ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/b/google-to-pay-settlement-to-illinois-residents-for-bipa-violations)); Texas collected $1.4B from Meta and $1.375B from Google under CUBI ([Linklaters](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102kb5i/the-price-of-privacy-compliance-what-googles-texas-settlement-signals-for-big-t)); the Dutch DPA fined Clearview EUR 30.5M under GDPR ([Pinsent Masons](https://pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/dutch-pa-fines-facial-recognition-company-gdpr-breach)). Bystanders cannot consent. Licensing adds friction: InsightFace code is MIT but its pretrained models are non-commercial without a licence; a packager claims YuNet plus SFace are Apache-2.0 (verify) ([InsightFace licensing](https://www.insightface.ai/solutions/face-recognition-licensing), [face-detect-gguf](https://huggingface.co/mudler/face-detect-gguf)). The conservative design is no identity matching: detect faces and count people on-device, offer at most an on-device "me vs not me" flag from the user's own selfie, store no templates server-side, and treat any face or voice embedding as potentially biometric. India's DPDP Rules core duties start around 13 May 2027, so build DPDP-ready now ([JSA Prism](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/JSA-Prism-InfoTech-November-2025-DPDP-Rules.Final_.pdf)).

**AI labelling, app stores and vendor terms.** If the product only cuts, reorders and captions real footage, labelling duty is lighter; generated B-roll, lip-sync or voice synthesis triggers it. EU AI Act Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026 with a machine-readable marking grace period to 2 December 2026 for existing generative systems (dates vary between secondary sources) ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force)); India's synthetic-content labelling amendment took effect 20 February 2026 ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content)); California's AI Transparency Act became operative on 2 August 2026 per secondary sources. Embed C2PA metadata on generative exports. Apple's November 2025 guideline 5.1.2(i) requires explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI, so uploading footage to Gemini or TwelveLabs needs a just-in-time consent screen naming the vendor, plus in-app account deletion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)). Use paid API tiers only: paid Gemini does not train on your data, OpenAI logs up to 30 days, and zero retention needs Vertex AI or OpenAI approval ([Gemini ZDR doc](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/zdr.md.txt), [OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). Several legal areas were never reached: the ELVIS Act and other state replica laws, the Instagram Terms scraping clause, TikTok official terms, GDPR guidance, and Runway terms.

## Build the cheap deterministic core first, and kill the idea early if coverage is low

**Recommended stack.** Take the reference as a user-supplied file or share-sheet video, with a rights attestation and ephemeral analysis. Run TransNetV2 or PySceneDetect for cuts, OpenCV flow plus tracking for camera and subject motion, and Gemini 3.8 Flash (or Pegasus 1.6 in an A/B on ten hand-labelled reels) with a JSON schema per shot clip for shot size, angle, action, on-screen text and transitions, assembling the ShotSpec. Index the library at shot level with on-device keyframe embeddings (MobileCLIP2-S0 is about 1.5 ms per image on iPhone-class hardware, though Apple's model terms need a commercial check) and cloud embeddings of 360-480p proxies (Gemini Embedding 2 or Marengo 3.5), VLM tags as structured columns, pgvector with a user_id partition, and a VLM rerank of the top 10-20 ([apple/ml-mobileclip](https://github.com/apple/ml-mobileclip)). Classify each slot exact, similar or missing with hard gates on orientation, duration and (optionally) person, soft scores on shot size and direction, and a quality prior. Plan with an LLM that emits timeline JSON and patch operations; render per-shot cached with FFmpeg (add Remotion only for animated captions, after checking the licence); caption with Scribe or faster-whisper; snap cuts to beats with Beat This or librosa; use the user's own audio, a licensed API, or publish-time Instagram audio rather than the reference's track. Keep originals on-device and reference them by local identifier, using PHPicker or the Android Photo Picker to ease store review. Cap verify-and-repair at three loops and cap chat revisions to bound cost. Defer generative fill, face matching and link-paste ingestion until the core retains users.

**Validation plan, in order.** First, spend a day trying invideo's reference-matching agent on ten real reels; the notes did no hands-on testing of any competitor, so the single biggest unknown is how well the closest product already works. Second, run the offline library test: 20 volunteers each supply a library and ten trending reels, run the pipeline with an off-the-shelf embedding plus an LLM judge, and measure the share of reference shots with an acceptable match. The notes' example kill threshold is **under 40% coverage on libraries of two hours or more**, and that threshold is a starting point, not a derived number. Third, run a concierge test: send ten small businesses a shot-list PDF or WhatsApp message and measure what share film and return footage within 48 hours; this directly tests the cold-start path that matters most. Fourth, compare AI-matched cuts against CapCut/Edits template swaps with blind raters, since templates already handle timing. Fifth, only then launch a narrow paid pilot in one vertical with a per-Reel or capped price and a retention target of two or more Reels per month per active user at month three, benchmarked against the 21.1% annual AI-app retention. Monitor CapCut and Instagram Edits release notes monthly for reference-to-timeline or library-search features, which would move the white space under you.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that the product's thesis lives in the gap analysis, not in the editing: editing from a reference is already commodity (invideo, CapCut, Edits), AI-powered library search is being funded (Clipto, Shade), and the cheap, deterministic render stack means a competitor can copy a prototype quickly. What a competitor cannot copy quickly is a user-confirmed, accumulating index plus a trustworthy "you have these, film these" loop, and only if the coverage numbers and the willingness to film hold up. Those two numbers decide the idea more than any model choice.

Two decisions follow that the original concept does not make. Treat "no good match" and "ask the user to shoot" as the primary experience, not an edge case, and price per outcome, because credit-opacity complaints and 21.1% annual retention punish usage-metered AI subscriptions. And scope out of the first release the two features that carry the most legal and cost exposure, face identity matching and link-pasted references, since the first invites BIPA/GDPR/CUBI liability and the second breaches platform terms.
